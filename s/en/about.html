I Will Not Look Away · 2026

About

One person alone. No organisation, no movement — a citizen who chose not to stay silent.

This site is written by one person. I do not represent an organisation, a party or a movement. I am a European citizen who travelled widely for work and met people of every background: social, religious, political, geographical. I have Jewish, Muslim, Catholic and atheist friends. This diversity is not a backdrop to my life: it is how I learned to think.

Where it began

The site was born for Gaza. On that conflict I listened to everyone, starting with those closest to me. My Jewish friends put me to the test, and I tried seriously to understand their point of view. In the end I did not find arguments, but justifications for acts that international law defines as unlawful. Staying silent, so as not to hurt a friendship or not to seem what I am not, would have been dishonest: towards them even before towards myself.

And here is the point. Writing about Gaza, I had to ask myself each time what we would say if someone else committed the same acts. The question did not stop there.

What it has become

Today the site follows international law wherever it is broken, whoever breaks it: the wars in Sudan, Ukraine, Myanmar, DR Congo, Yemen and the Sahel; Iran and the United States; Europe's choices on energy, cars and immigration; the International Criminal Court under pressure; the death penalty; artificial intelligence at war. Many of these crises stay out of the news. Alongside the pieces are the tools to read them on your own: Country profiles and Glossary, Legal references, ICC Fugitives, Map. All in eleven languages, because these rules apply to everyone and everyone must be able to read them.

What I believe

I believe in international law not as a rhetorical convenience, but as the real foundation of living together. Those rules were written after the Second World War so that certain things would not happen again. When those with enough power or enough money get around them without consequences, those who come after learn that the rules apply only to the weak. I believe in Western morality in its oldest and most uncomfortable sense: the morality of people, not that of politics and economics.

Editorial honesty

This site is not neutral and does not pretend to be. I ask to be judged on method. In the Analysis pieces, facts, dated and sourced, are kept apart from judgement, which is always declared. Every piece must pass the same test: would the condemnation hold if the parties were swapped? When I get something wrong, the correction stays visible in the piece, with its date. Opinions, Manifestos and Petitions are the openly campaigning part.

For a long time the coverage was weighted towards Israel and Palestine, the subject where it all began. I started correcting this with an explicit protocol for choosing what to follow, explained on the Method page.
