When an international court rules or a UN commission concludes an inquiry, everyone talks about it for a day. Then the document disappears, and what remains are the sentences quoted in newspapers. Here we collect the documents we cite in our pieces, with a link to the original text, so that anyone can read what they actually say.

For each document we state who wrote it, when, what it established and with what degree of certainty. Words matter. A judgment of the International Court of Justice binds the States that are parties to the case. The report of a commission of inquiry is not a judgment: it says there are “reasonable grounds to believe” that a crime has been committed, and asks a court to rule. When the accused State has responded, we report its response too.

This page grows with the site: every new piece that cites a document adds it here.

UN Commission of Inquiry on the Occupied Palestinian Territory and Israel · 14 October 2026 · Israel/Palestine

West Bank: extermination and other crimes against humanity (A/81/430)

What it establishesIsraeli authorities and forces have committed in the West Bank the crimes against humanity of extermination, torture, rape and other sexual violence, and persecution, and continue to commit acts of genocide in Gaza despite the ceasefire. Since October 2023 Israeli forces have been responsible for about 95% of the Palestinians killed in the West Bank. The report also examines the Palestinian security forces: at least 14 Palestinians killed between October 2023 and October 2025, according to the UN Human Rights Office, and arbitrary arrests, torture and ill-treatment of journalists, activists and human rights defenders, with court release orders ignored. For the Palestinian Authority the Commission finds violations of the right to life and a failure to conduct prompt and effective investigations, not international crimes.

Degree of certaintyFinding of the Commission, presented to the General Assembly. Advance version, not yet edited.

ResponseIsrael rejects the findings: the Foreign Ministry called it “a propaganda document detached from reality”. We have not found a response by the Palestinian Authority to the report.

Independent International Fact-Finding Mission on the Islamic Republic of Iran · 17 September 2026 · Iran

Iran: repression of the protests and war with the United States and Israel (A/HRC/63/61)

What it establishesIn repressing the protests that began in December 2025, the Iranian authorities committed crimes against humanity. For the war that began on 28 February 2026, there are reasonable grounds to believe that the United States committed war crimes in two strikes that killed at least 178 civilians: the Tomahawk missiles on the primary school in Minab, with more than 150 dead including about 120 children, and the strike on a sports complex and residential area in Lamerd, with 22 dead.

Degree of certainty“Reasonable grounds to believe”; a body of inquiry, not a court. The mission has never had access to Iran.

ResponseThe State Department does not consider the report credible; the Pentagon says its own investigation is still under way. The United States denies striking in the vicinity of Lamerd.

UN Assistance Mission in Afghanistan (UNAMA) · 12 May 2026 · Afghanistan

Afghanistan: civilian casualties of the clashes with Pakistan, January–March 2026

What it establishesIn the first three months of 2026 clashes between Taliban and Pakistani forces killed 372 civilians in Afghanistan and injured 397. Pakistani airstrikes caused 64% of the casualties. More than half of the deaths came from a single attack, the strike of 16 March on the Omid drug treatment centre in Kabul: 269 killed and 122 injured.

Degree of certaintyData verified by the mission.

ResponseIn its written response included in the report, Pakistan says no hospital, treatment centre or civilian facility was targeted, and that 130 Pakistani civilians and security personnel have been killed since the start of the year.
