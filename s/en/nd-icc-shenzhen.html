Three signatures against the Court: in Shenzhen, Washington, Moscow and Beijing call for immunity for those who govern

19 November 2026 — Shenzhen, China

Series “The Court under siege” · 6 of 7

In brief

  • On 19 November 2026 in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the APEC summit, the presidents of the United States, China and Russia held the first three-way meeting in history and signed a four-point "Declaration toward Peace".
  • Point 3 states that state consent and respect for the immunities of state officials are "of paramount importance" and that the ICC and similar bodies "must not abuse their jurisdiction".
  • At the opening, Trump called the Court "out of control", the same words he used in September at the UN General Assembly.
  • None of the three countries is a member of the Court. Putin has faced an arrest warrant since March 2023 for the deportation of Ukrainian children.
  • The declaration is political and has no legal effect on the Court. The UN, the European Union and Italy defended the ICC.
  • Until October, Moscow and Beijing kept silent on the US sanctions against the Court. Now there is a shared text.

What happened

On Thursday 19 November, in a villa on the outskirts of Shenzhen, Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin sat at the same table for about eighty minutes. It had never happened before. At the end they released a four-point "Declaration toward Peace", published in full by the Kremlin and by Chinese state media, including the broadcaster CGTN.

The first two points concern reform of the UN, which the three commit to pursue as permanent members of the Security Council, and an increase in the production of refined fuels. The fourth repeats that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought and announces a dialogue on nuclear risks, with no timetable and no cuts to arsenals.

The third point concerns international justice. The three heads of state recognise that "the principle of state consent and respect for the immunities of state officials are of paramount importance" and agree that "the International Criminal Court and other similar bodies must not abuse their jurisdiction".

Trump had previewed it when opening the meeting. He said he would suggest "ways to protect our nations' sovereignty from rogue globalist organizations such as the International Criminal Court, which has gotten out of control", and which in his view has "really strayed far from beyond its jurisdiction". He added: "I believe we all believe" it (The New Republic). It was the same wording he used in September before the General Assembly, covered in The Appeal That Fell Flat.

The text does not mention Ukraine, nor the war between the United States and Iran.

What it is worth

The declaration is not a treaty. It binds no state, does not amend the Rome Statute and does not touch any of the proceedings open in The Hague. The Statute can only be changed by the states that are party to it, and none of the three signatories is: the United States and Russia had signed it and then withdrew their signatures, and China never signed it.

Its weight is political. The US sanctions against the Court, covered in Six months for the Court, were Washington's campaign, and Russia and China watched without taking a position. From today, the three largest military powers outside the Court have put in writing a shared principle on what the Court may and may not do. In Shenzhen it was also signed by a man wanted by that Court: on 17 March 2023 the ICC issued an arrest warrant against Putin for the unlawful deportation of children from the occupied Ukrainian territories. Moscow rejects the accusations.

Reactions

  • UN: the Secretary-General's deputy spokesperson, Farhan Haq, said the Court is "one of the crucial bodies for international justice" and must be strengthened, not weakened. Asked how leaders, at least one of whom is wanted, could make decisions about the Court, he said he would leave it to the Court to discuss its own work (Anadolu).
  • European Union: it reaffirmed its "firm and unwavering commitment" to the rules-based international order, "of which the International Criminal Court is a fundamental part" (Sky TG24).
  • Italy: Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani said the Court "can make mistakes, but we cannot call into question an institution" like the ICC, and that Shenzhen "is not the new Yalta" (Agenzia Nova).
  • Ukraine: Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha wrote that "Shenzhen is not the Yalta of Putin's dreams". Zelensky, who was not invited, called it a mistake to meet Putin without Ukraine (Silere non possum).
  • Analysts: for Jonathan Eyal (RUSI), the attack on international courts is the most significant aspect of the summit: "the great powers dictate, the small ones submit".
  • The Court: at the time of publication it had not commented.

Legal reading

The immunity the declaration speaks of is real, but it applies in a specific setting. A sitting head of state cannot be tried by the courts of another state: among equals, no one judges the other. The International Court of Justice established this in 2002 in the Arrest Warrant case (Congo v. Belgium). In the same judgment, however, the Court made two points. Immunity is not impunity: the crime remains a crime. And immunity does not prevent trial before an international tribunal that has jurisdiction.

The Rome Statute goes in the same direction. Article 27 says that official capacity, including that of head of state, does not exempt a person from responsibility and does not bar the Court from proceeding. In 2019 the ICC Appeals Chamber, in the case of former Sudanese president al-Bashir, ruled that head-of-state immunity does not apply before an international court.

That leaves "state consent". The Court does not try just anyone: it has jurisdiction over crimes committed on the territory of a state that has accepted it (Article 12), even if the accused is a national of a country that never joined. This is the case with Putin. The alleged acts took place in Ukraine, which had accepted the Court's jurisdiction through two declarations in 2014 and 2015 and became the 125th State Party on 1 January 2025. It is also the case with Netanyahu, for the events in Gaza, where Palestine has been a State Party since 2015 and the Court has rejected Israel's challenges. Asking for the consent of the accused's state to count, rather than that of the state where the crime took place, amounts to asking for those warrants not to exist.

The other side's strongest argument. The 2019 ruling on immunity is contested by part of legal scholarship, because it applies to heads of state of countries that did not sign the Statute a rule those countries did not accept. On this point, the Shenzhen declaration touches an open question among jurists, not a baseless claim.

The symmetry test

The Shenzhen principle protects all three signatories, in different ways.

  • Moscow: it protects Putin directly.
  • Washington: it protects an ally, Netanyahu, wanted since November 2024, and its own personnel in the Afghanistan investigation. Since 2021 that part of the investigation has not been a priority for the Prosecutor's Office, which focuses on the Taliban and the Islamic State, but it is not closed, and Washington is demanding that it be closed. In 2023 the same Washington, which does not recognise the Court, had called the warrant against Putin "justified": President Biden said so.
  • Beijing: it has no open proceedings, but it strengthens a principle that shields it in the future.

The reverse also holds. The Court has issued warrants against Africans, Russians, Israelis, Taliban leaders and the former Philippine president Duterte, but never against nationals of the Western powers. It is a criticism the Court has faced for years, including from member states, and five of them (Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, Venezuela and Chad) have started or announced their withdrawal. That criticism, however, asks for a Court that judges everyone. The Shenzhen text asks for a Court that does not judge those who govern.

Our view

This section is a declared opinion and is kept separate from the facts.

If two people agree not to report each other, theft does not stop being theft: they have only decided that neither of them will judge it. But if the theft happened in someone else's house, and the owner has called the police, their agreement does not count.

That is what happened in Shenzhen. No one said "let's abolish the Court". They said something subtler: the Court is fine as long as it does not touch those who govern. Put that way, it sounds reasonable. But if it became the rule, it would hollow out Article 27, which is the reason the Court was created. Three countries that are not part of the Court have tried to change the rules of a field they do not play on. The rules of that field can only be changed by the 125 states that play on it, and in December they meet in The Hague.

What to watch

  • The response of the Court and its Presidency.
  • The Assembly of States Parties in December 2026: whether the "dialogue with non-member states" starts from this text.
  • The "proposals" announced by Trump: whether they become concrete acts.
  • The 7 April 2027 expiry of the US Treasury licence that keeps the Court running.
  • Whether the EU activates its blocking statute.
